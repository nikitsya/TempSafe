# Usability Test Script — Core TempSafe Scenario

## Purpose

This script describes the usability test carried out on 5 November 2026. It
focuses on whether a user can understand the current condition of the
Controlled Drug Box and find information about an earlier temperature
excursion. It does not cover every feature of the application.

## Participants and setup

Two proxy users took part, using the working TempSafe website on a laptop.
They were not paramedics. The test used real temperature readings from the
SCD41 sensor, recorded and stored by the system.

Before the sessions, the facilitator prepared one temperature event by
warming the sensor and allowing it to return to the Normal range. Both
participants were shown the same event. They did not watch the sensor being
warmed or cooled; they were shown the website after the temperature had
returned to Normal.

The scenario mentioned a notification as context. This script does not
claim that a real phone notification was delivered during the test.

## Before the tasks

The facilitator explained that the team was testing the application, not the
participants, and that there were no right or wrong actions. Participants
were asked to use the interface as they thought appropriate and, where
possible, explain their thoughts aloud. Both consented to written notes.

## Scenario

The facilitator explained the following situation:

> You are an Advanced Paramedic treating a patient away from the ambulance.
> A temperature notification arrives, but you cannot respond immediately.
> You open TempSafe after the temperature has returned to the Normal range.
> You need to understand the current condition of the Controlled Drug Box
> and find out what happened while you were busy.

The facilitator did not explain the navigation or name the pages and buttons
to use before the tasks.

## Tasks

### T1 — Understand the current status

**Instruction:** Determine the current condition of the Controlled Drug Box.

Observe whether the participant finds the current temperature and status,
understands that the reading is in the Normal range and needs any assistance.

### T2 — Check whether an excursion happened

**Instruction:** Find out whether a temperature problem occurred while you
were busy.

Do not direct the participant to History. Observe where they look, whether
they try to find an earlier event and whether they can identify it. Record an
attempt separately from successfully determining what happened. Note if the
participant assumes that the current Normal status means no earlier problem
occurred.

### T3 — Investigate the previous event

**Instruction:** Find the previous temperature event and say when it
happened, how long it lasted and the highest temperature reached.

Observe whether the participant can find History and identify the event,
its time, estimated duration, peak temperature and date. Record any
information that is difficult to find or appears to be missing. A duration
derived from periodic readings is an estimate rather than an exact crossing
time.

## Recording observations

For each task, record the outcome, assistance, difficulties and participant
comments on the observation sheet:

- **Y:** completed without help.
- **H:** completed with help.
- **N:** not completed or the participant gave up.

An unexpected navigation path is not itself an error if the participant
completes the task. Do not treat an attempted search as a completed task
unless the participant finds the requested information.

## Details section

Details was not a separate task in T1–T3. After the main tasks, the
facilitator asked participants about the section and discussed its contents
with them. Observations and feedback are recorded with the session results.

## Rating questions

After the tasks, participants rated each question from **1 to 5**:
1 means very difficult or unclear; 5 means very easy or clear.

1. How easy was it to understand the current temperature status?
2. How clear was the current temperature display?
3. How easy was it to identify whether a temperature excursion happened while you were away?
4. How easy was it to find previous temperature alerts?
5. How easy was it to use the History page?
6. How clear was the duration of a temperature event?
7. How easy was it to identify the date and time of a temperature excursion?
8. How clear was the peak temperature information?
9. How easy was it to navigate between Current Status and History?
10. How clear was the TempSafe interface overall?
11. How confident did you feel completing the task?

## Open feedback questions

1. What was the easiest part of the task?
2. What was the hardest part of the task?
3. Was it clear what the condition is now and what happened earlier?
4. Was any information unclear, unnecessary or repetitive?
5. What would you change or improve?

## Results

Record what each participant tried to do, any difficulty, assistance given,
and proposed changes in a separate results document. Distinguish observed
behaviour from participant feedback and the team's proposed changes.
